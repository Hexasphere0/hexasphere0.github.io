Today, I made a lot of progress on the game. I started the day by debugging the gravity logic. The core physics was already complete, but I had to work on the inputs. I started by reworking the grounded logic, but it was not very simple. The first issue is that the grounded raycasts only point downwards, meaning it doesn't adjust with gravity. For example, if the gravity was upside down, you wouldn't be able to jump as you would expect. To solve this, I calculated a down vector and multiplied the raycast direction by that. However, this led to another strange issue: The grounded logic only worked when the player was at the left or right of a block, and didn't work vertically. I spent a long time debugging this, but the most common issues related to raycasting didn't apply. I finally figured out that the rays were not long enough by randomly changing variables. I couldn't figure out this issue, because the rays I was drawing for debugging had a fixed length of 1, while the real raycasts used a parameter I created a long time ago.

The next thing I did was orient the camera to point in the direction of gravity. This makes levels where you rotate around a center of gravity feel a lot more natural. However, this will probably feel unnatural if the gravity simply flips 180, so I will have to disable the rotation in that case. Next, me and my friend decided to do a level creation contest, because we haven't created a level in a long time. 

The prompt we settled on was "exploration", and I am super happy with how my level turned out. My level kind of works like a puzzle, where you have to meticulously search every part of the level in order to find the checkpoint to win the level. There are plenty of dead ends, however I didn't want to create boring dead ends. Therefore, I made it so there are jumps that feel possible but are actually just out of reach, meaning the player has to figure out which ones are real dead ends and which ones have real gameplay. The "fake" gameplay that seems inaccessible is often a hint as well. For example, there is one section in the level that seems impossible to reach, but can actually be reached by falling down from another section of the level. There are also several places that look like dead ends, but can actually lead to various ideas if the player takes the correct actions. I tried to make sure that the player always had hints as to where to go, and wasn't just guessing what to do.
