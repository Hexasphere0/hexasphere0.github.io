Today, I am writing about deckbuilding in card games, and what makes for a good deckbuilding game. To start, I need to define some terms. First of all, I am only talking about the concept of creating new deck ideas, not optimizing existing decks. You can optimize decks in pretty much every game and format, but only a select few have good deckbuilding. 

The first, and most obvious factor is having a high quality card pool. This isn't just the same as a big card pool, instead I'm only talking about the number of playable cards. For example, in many games with a long history, even though they have 20 thousand cards, only a small percent of those are actually playable. Let me define playable really quick: the cards that you could consider putting in your deck that aren't outclassed by another card. 

The second, and maybe more controversial point is that I don't think having a lot of cards that do the same thing result in a higher quality card pool. For example, in Magic, you can see the same effect across a ton of different cards. Some effects are printed every set, such as a red damage dealing spell. However, do these cards really lead to that much intrigue? I don't know.  

The biggest pull of deckbuilding, and something most games already do, is that the deeper you go into an "archetype", the more powerful cards get.
